 ## Feature Project
- Hack The Box (HTB) "Guardian" Machine (Hard Difficulty)
  
  Role: Primary Solver
  
  Date: 2025

  This project was assigned as part of my
university coursework, where our team was
challenged to compromise two Hack The Box
(HTB) machines, including one on the 'Hard' difficulty. Initially, I felt quite daunted by the
'Guardian' machine. My previous Capture
The Flag (CTF) experience was primarily
built on picoCTF, and stepping into the HTB
environment for the first time was a
significant leap. However, I decided to take
on the challenge as the primary solver for
this machine. To overcome the learning curve, I dedicated
my time to researching attack vectors online
and discussing strategies with my friends. Through persistence and continuous learning,
I successfully exploited multiple
vulnerabilities and captured several critical
flags. Although I reached about 90%
completion rather than fully rooting the
machine, this experience was a major
milestone. It pushed my technical boundaries, sharpened my analytical skills, and taught
me how to adapt quickly to entirely new
cybersecurity environments.

- picoCTF Security Challenges & Write-Up Documentation

  Role: CTF Participant & Write-Up Author

  Date: 2025

  This project started as an assignment for my
Penetration Testing course, challenging us to
solve easy to medium labs on the picoCTF
platform. Since I had zero prior experience
with CTFs, it was honestly quite
overwhelming at first. However, by actively
researching online and discussing with my
friends, I slowly managed to get the hang of
it. This hands on process allowed me to
myself with essential tools like Wireshark, CyberChef, and Burp Suite, while also
helping me discover a strong personal
interest in the Digital Forensics category. Beyond just solving the challenges and
finding the flags, this project taught me a
crucial lesson about the importance of
documentation in cybersecurity. I started
creating detailed Write-Ups for every forensic
challenge I completed, which trained me to
translate my raw technical steps into clear, structured reports. Ultimately, this experience
didn't just improve my technical problem- solving skills; it also built a solid foundation in
how to document findings professionally, a
skill I now realize is absolutely critical for any
security analyst.
